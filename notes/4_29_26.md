## Digital Elevation Models (DEMs)
- Digital surface models (DSMs)
- Digital terrain models (DTMs)
- Gonna kinda use these interchangeably as we don't really care about the difference
- Grids (rasters) of Earth's surface
    - Single band (m) (with respect to some reference point like "sea level")

### InSAR
- How do we create them?
    - SAR interferometry = we have some temporal baseline between two scenes, pretty much in the same orbital pattern and look gemoetry, have amplitude and phase to calculate interferogram
    1. Single pass approach = just have one sensor, multiple antennas on the same spacecraft
    - Ex. SRTM (antenna on the bottom of the spacecraft and an arm? I'm not really sure what he's saying. Somehow maps to surface topograpgy. Shuttle radar topografy mission.)
    - Spatial baseline
    2. Repeat pass approach = multiple collects from slightly different orbital tracks
    - Temporal baseline
    - Ex. Sentinel-1, Airbus, TerraSAR/TanDEM
- Phase difference is proportional to the topography of Earth (due to structural features on the ground)
- Phase unwrapping is necessary when difference spans into the next wave cycle (requires like trial-and-error stuff)
- Geocoding = map phase differences to actual elevation numbers and then reproject from sensor space -> ground space (geometric correction and gridding)

### Stereo photogrammetry
- Multiple captures with optical sensors
- Multiple views -> 3D reconstruction
- Typically done at a smaller scale (like flying a drone or something) to do really particular types of mapping

### LiDAR
- Light detection and ranging
- Fire lasers and measure the reflected light
- Gives extremely detailed elevation maps
- 3DEP program -> gridded elevation data at 1m or better (goddamn)
- Some states will even give you the lidar point cloud data
- Very expensive and hard to fly at scale
- Point clouds -> raster grids

## Visualizing elevation data
- Hillshade
    - Shaded relief = technique where lighting effect is added to a map based on variations in the landscaep
    - Place a virtual sun in the sky above the DEM (digital elevation map) 
    - Then calculate how light will travel from the source across the topography (cast shadows)
    - Azimuth = compass direction of light source (315 degrees)
    - Altitude - angle of light source above the horizon (45 degrees)

- What dictates water color?
    - Particles floating in it (sediments, plankton, seaweed)
    - Dissolved molecules (coffee tea)
    - Water itself
    - Bubbles
- Each absorbs some light, scatters the rest

- Deep water appears blue -> reflects
    - Inherent absorption properties (IAPs)
    - Multiple optical components => complex retrievals
    - Higher spectral resolution

- Apparent optical properties (AOPs)
    - How light is observed interacting with water
    - Changes in incoming light and direction of observation
